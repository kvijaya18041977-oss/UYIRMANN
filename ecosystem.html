Agriculture × Nature

A Farm Is An Ecosystem.

A field is never just a field. Plants, soil, insects, birds, water, trees and people interact every day to create a living agricultural landscape.

Explore the living field
01 / Living Agriculture

Farming depends on more life than we see.

A healthy agricultural landscape contains many forms of life. Pollinators help plants reproduce, birds interact with insects, soil organisms support soil processes, trees provide habitat, and wetlands connect water with biodiversity.

01 🐝

Pollinators

Bees and other pollinating animals help many flowering plants complete their reproductive cycles.

02 🐦

Birds

Birds are part of agricultural food webs and can interact with insects, seeds and plants around farms.

03 🦋

Beneficial Insects

Many insects play important ecological roles, including pollination and relationships within food webs.

04 🌳

Trees & Hedges

Trees and vegetation can provide shade, shelter and habitat while adding diversity to agricultural landscapes.

05 🪱

Soil Life

Roots, microorganisms and soil animals contribute to the complex living system beneath the field.

06 💧

Wetlands

Wetlands and water bodies can provide habitat while connecting agriculture with wider landscape and water systems.

03 / Nature at Work

Nature Works Quietly.

Much of an ecosystem's work happens without being noticed. Pollination, decomposition, nutrient cycling, habitat and predator-prey relationships continuously connect living organisms with the agricultural landscape.

04 / Ecosystem Connections

What biodiversity gives the field.

Healthy ecosystems support functions that are valuable to agriculture and the wider environment.

🌼 Pollination

Pollinating organisms connect flowers, plants and reproduction.

⚖️ Natural Balance

Food webs create relationships among plants, insects, birds and other organisms.

🌱 Soil Health

Soil organisms participate in the living processes of the soil.

💧 Water Retention

Vegetation and healthy landscapes can influence how water moves through an environment.

🏡 Habitat

Trees, field edges and wetlands can create spaces for wildlife.

🕸️ Food Webs

Every field contains relationships between producers, consumers and decomposers.

05 / Beyond The Crop

The crop is only one part of the story.

When we look beyond the crop itself, the agricultural landscape becomes a network of relationships. Field boundaries, trees, water bodies, soil organisms and wildlife can all contribute to the character of a farm.

01 Protect habitats wherever possible.
02 Reduce unnecessary disturbance to living systems.
03 Support native plants and local biodiversity.
04 Observe nature before making decisions.
Field Observation

Look closer.

A farmer can see more than a crop. The field can reveal insects moving between plants, birds crossing the landscape, changes in soil moisture, flowering plants and signs of seasonal change.

Observation turns a farm into a living classroom.

06 / Future Landscapes

Agriculture can grow with nature.

The future of farming can combine ecological understanding, traditional knowledge and modern technology.

🌳 Agroforestry

Combining trees with agricultural landscapes can create more diverse farming environments.

💧 Wetland Protection

Protecting wetlands helps preserve important connections between water, habitat and biodiversity.

🦋 Habitat Corridors

Connected habitats can help living organisms move through wider agricultural landscapes.

🪱 Soil Biodiversity

Understanding life below the surface can improve how we think about healthy agricultural soils.

📡 Smart Monitoring

Sensors and digital tools can help observe environmental conditions across farms.

🌍 Climate Resilience

Diverse agricultural landscapes can be part of strategies for adapting to changing environmental conditions.
