01 — The Connection

Without water, agriculture cannot thrive.

Agriculture depends on water at many stages — from preparing the land and establishing crops to supporting growth and producing a harvest.

Tamil Nadu has a diverse water landscape. Rivers, reservoirs, traditional tanks, canals, rainfall and groundwater all contribute to the agricultural story in different regions.

Water is more than a resource.
It connects climate, soil, crops, farmers, ecosystems and food.
02 — The Water Network

Many paths.
One essential resource.

Water reaches agricultural landscapes through different natural and managed systems. Each plays a different role in the farming environment.

🌊

Rivers

Rivers carry freshwater across landscapes and have supported farming communities for generations.

🏞️

Tanks

Traditional tanks can store seasonal water and form an important part of Tamil Nadu's agricultural heritage.

💧

Canals

Canals can move stored or diverted water toward agricultural areas and connect water sources with fields.

🌧️

Rainfall

Rain is one of the most important natural inputs in agriculture, directly and indirectly supporting crops and water storage.

🌍

Groundwater

Water stored below the ground can support farming where groundwater resources are available.

🪣

Farm Storage

Ponds and other storage systems can help retain water within agricultural landscapes for later use.

04 — Water Wisdom

Ancient systems.
Modern questions.

Tamil agricultural landscapes have long included systems designed around seasonal rainfall, water storage and distribution.

Today, agriculture also uses modern technologies to observe soil moisture, weather conditions and irrigation needs. Traditional knowledge and modern tools can both help us understand the relationship between water and farming.

🏞️ Tank Systems Water storage has been an important feature of many agricultural landscapes in Tamil Nadu.
🌧️ Seasonal Understanding Farming traditions developed around changing rainfall and seasonal agricultural rhythms.
📡 Modern Monitoring Sensors and digital systems can provide new ways to observe water and field conditions.
